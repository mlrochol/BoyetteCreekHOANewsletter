Vehicles

  • Boats, trailers, campers and RVs must be kept inside the garage.
  • A vehicle that doesn't run can't stay outside more than 48 hours unless it's out of view.
  • Our streets are county roads, so street parking is handled by the Sheriff's Office, not the HOA. Please keep sidewalks, mailboxes and driveways clear.

Trash & bulk items

Days set by the county and occasionally changed. Confirm at HCFLGov.net/Trash.

  • Put cans at the curb no earlier than the evening before pickup, and bring them back by the evening of pickup day.
  • Between pickups, keep cans out of sight in the garage or behind ARB-approved screening.
  • Bulky items (furniture, mattresses, appliances): call County Solid Waste at (813) 272-5680 to schedule a pickup before anything goes to the curb. A per-item fee applies. You can also haul items to a county drop-off site yourself.

Welcome, neighbor!

Boyette Creek is a deed-restricted community of 556 homes. The Homeowners Association is run by a volunteer Board of resident Directors with day-to-day help from our management company, McNeil Management Services. Every lot is covered by the same recorded rules, and they move with the house when it sells or is rented.

This guide covers what most new residents ask about in their first few months. It's a summary, so the full documents are on the HOA website.

Your first-month checklist

  • Create your Homeowner Portal account at mcneilmsi.com to view your account and pay dues.
  • Give McNeil your contact info. There's one Address of Record per lot, so update it whenever your phone, email or mailing address changes.
  • Read the Declaration and ARB Planning Criteria on boyettecreekhoa.com before you plan any outside project.
  • Follow neighborhood news on our Linktree. It has the Boyette Creek Buzz newsletter, events, the neighbor business directory and a form for messaging the Board.
  • Renting? Your landlord is required to give you a copy of the community rules. Ask for it.

Dues are billed twice a year, due January 1 and July 1. Each December, owners get a notice of the next year's assessment. If yours hasn't arrived by year-end, contact McNeil.

Good to know

Renting & storms

If you rent out your home

  • Only whole-home rentals are allowed, with no room-by-room leasing. Leases must run from 30 days to 1 year, so short-term and vacation rentals (Airbnb, VRBO and similar) are not allowed.
  • Give the HOA (through McNeil) 30 days' written notice and a copy of the lease. The tenant also completes the HOA's information form before moving in.
  • Give your tenants a copy of the rules. Owners are responsible for their tenants following them.

Hurricane season

  • Put shutters up no more than 72 hours before a forecast storm, and take them down within 10 days after it passes.
  • Permanent shutters, standby generators and metal roofs need ARB approval. Metal roofs must be shingle-style in a traditional color.

Pets & neighbors

  • Pets must be leashed or inside a fenced yard, and owners pick up after them every time.
  • Keep noise, smoke and odors from bothering the neighbors.

Get involved

Board meetings are open to all homeowners, with dates on the website and Linktree. Volunteers run the Welcome and Beautification committees, Street Captains and the ARB. Scan to raise your hand!

Before you build, paint or plant

Your home & yard

Ask first. Any change to the outside of your home or lot needs written approval from the Architectural Review Board (ARB) before work starts. That includes paint, roof, fences, driveways, pools, landscaping beds, tree removal, shutters, generators and screen enclosures. Applications are on the HOA website and need a copy of your lot survey. The ARB responds in writing within 30 days.

Declaration Art. VII · ARB Planning Criteria

  • Paint: repaint in your original builder color or choose from the ARB-approved palette. Anything else needs a color chip submitted for review.
  • Lawns: front and side yards stay fully sodded, mowed and edged. That includes the grass strip between the sidewalk and the street.
  • Sidewalks: each owner maintains and repairs the sidewalk along their lot.
  • Driveways: thinking about widening yours or adding pavers? Get ARB approval first. Any work between the sidewalk and the street also needs a Hillsborough County permit.
  • Fences: wood or wood-look PVC, with posts on the inside, and never in front of the house. ARB approval is required.
  • Play sets and yard items go in the back yard. Basketball hoops need ARB approval.
  • Equipment like pool pumps, softeners and tanks must be screened from the street with shrubs or approved fencing.
  • Mailboxes: classic black metal box with a red flag on a black post. The bottom of the box sits 39" above the ground.
